Hiring non-EU workers in Romania in 2026: what changed with OUG 32/2026

A clear guide for employers: the new single application on WorkinRomania.gov.ro, D/AM1 and D/AM2 visas, employer and agency rules, and the steps to hire workers from outside the EU.

← All articlesPublished 17 October 2026 · 6 min read

Romanian employers have relied on workers from Nepal, India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and other countries for several years. In 2026 the rules changed. Emergency Ordinance OUG 32/2026, published on 27 April 2026, replaced the old system, and the new procedure has applied since 8 August 2026. Here is what employers need to know.

1. The old work permit is gone

Until this summer, an employer first obtained a work permit (aviz de muncă) from the General Inspectorate for Immigration (IGI), then the worker applied for a visa. That permit no longer exists for new cases. It is replaced by a single application (cerere unică) filed online on the national platform WorkinRomania.gov.ro.

2. Two types of work visa

  • D/AM1 is for special categories, for example highly qualified workers and, according to the new rules, full-time workers from the Republic of Moldova, Ukraine and Serbia.
  • D/AM2 covers most recruitment: permanent, seasonal and cross-border workers in occupations on the national list of shortage occupations (Lista ocupațiilor deficitare).

3. The steps for an employer

  1. Check the occupation. For D/AM2 the job must be on the current shortage occupations list.
  2. Register the company on WorkinRomania.gov.ro. The company must have no outstanding tax debts and no recent sanctions for undeclared work.
  3. Publish a firm job offer: position, duties, salary, working hours, contract length, workplace and any accommodation or transport.
  4. File the single application with the employment contract registered in REGES-ONLINE and the worker’s documents (passport, criminal record certificate, travel medical insurance and, where needed, proof of qualifications).
  5. The worker applies for the visa in person at the Romanian embassy or consulate after a positive decision.
  6. After arrival, the worker obtains the residence permit from IGI (biometrics and card).

4. Directly or through an agency?

For D/AM2 workers the general rule is recruitment through a placement agency authorised by ANOFM. An employer can recruit directly only with “authorised employer” status, which requires, among other things, at least 24 months of continuous activity, an average of at least 50 employees in the previous year, previous experience with foreign workers and a financial guarantee of EUR 1,000 per worker.

Agencies also face stricter rules: a specific ANOFM authorisation valid for two years, a financial guarantee, placement only in shortage occupations on the basis of firm job offers, and a three-party contract between agency, employer and worker.

5. New obligations after hiring

  • The employment contract must be in Romanian and in a language the worker understands. A contract only in Romanian can be fined 6,000 lei.
  • Pay and working conditions must be equal to those of Romanian employees.
  • Employers must inform the authorities about terminations and certain unexplained absences, and give workers information about accommodation, transport and integration.
  • A worker can generally change employer after at least six months.

What this means for your company

The new system is digital and, in principle, more transparent. It is also stricter: employers are checked more closely and mistakes in the offer or the contract can stop an application. Good preparation matters more than ever: a precise job offer, correct contracts in two languages, real accommodation plans and candidates whose documents are complete before the application is filed.
